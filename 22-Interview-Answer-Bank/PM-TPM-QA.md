# PM / TPM Interview Q&A

## How do you start a project?
Align sponsor, problem/outcome, scope boundaries, success measures, stakeholders, assumptions and major dependencies before detailed planning.

## How do you build a plan?
Decompose outcomes into deliverables, dependencies/milestones, team estimates and capacity; baseline after important assumptions are visible.

## How do you manage risk?
Separate risk from issue, assign owner, probability/impact, trigger and response; connect exposure to decisions.

## Scope creep?
Compare with baseline, clarify value, quantify scope/date/cost/resource/quality impact, present swap/defer/extend options and obtain decision.

## API dependency late?
Confirm exact blocker/forecast vs needed-by, use agreed contract/mock where safe, keep real integration open and escalate critical-path options.

## Estimation?
Team-owned estimates with explicit assumptions/dependencies, historical evidence, ranges/three-point estimates and progressive refinement.

## Status reporting?
Outcome/progress, forecast vs baseline, material RAID/dependencies, budget/quality and decisions needed—not an activity list.

## Metrics?
Use decision signals: milestone forecast, predictability, churn, flow/cycle, quality, budget/EAC, dependency and recovery signals. Never score individuals from one metric.

## Missed sprint commitments?
Separate original commitment from added/removed scope; inspect blockers, dependencies, estimation and quality work; fix system causes.

## Stakeholders?
Map influence, interest, decision rights and information needs; executives get impact/options/decisions, teams get owners/dependencies/acceptance.

## Escalation?
Impact, evidence/confidence, options/trade-offs, recommendation, decision required/by when and consequence of no decision.

## Deadline at risk?
Recalculate remaining critical path/confidence; present scope/date/resource/resequence options and protect required quality/security.

## Budget?
Baseline + actuals + remaining forecast → EAC/variance; explain drivers and connect spend to outcomes.

## Change requests?
Document reason/value, impacts, alternatives/recommendation, authorized decision, then update baseline/communication.

## Quality?
Risk-based critical journeys, acceptance/NFRs, test/release gates, escaped defects and production signals; whole-team ownership.

## Release?
Scope/acceptance, integration, defects, compatibility/migrations, dependencies, observability, support, rollout and mitigation; human go/no-go.

## Production incident?
Protect users/data, establish owner, stabilize, gather evidence, communicate facts, validate recovery, then RCA/prevention.

## Good RCA?
Evidence-based impact/timeline/root cause/contributors/safeguard gaps plus specific owned corrective actions and validation.

## Stakeholder conflict?
Shared outcome → facts vs preferences → constraints/decision rights → options/evidence → accountable decision.

## Prioritization?
Value, urgency, risk, dependencies, cost of delay and capacity within organizational governance; make trade-offs explicit.

## Resources?
Compare skill/demand with capacity/timing; identify bottlenecks; resequence/scope/staff; don't use utilization as individual scoring.

## Project closure?
Acceptance, transition, open-item disposition, final schedule/financial state, lessons, benefits follow-up and closure approval.

## Multiple projects?
Portfolio priorities, milestones/dependencies, capacity conflicts and decision cadence; standardize visibility, not every execution detail.

## Working with engineering?
Bring product/delivery constraints; technical owners provide options/evidence/trade-offs; TPM makes technical risk visible without replacing architecture ownership.

## AI as PM/TPM?
Evidence synthesis, meeting/actions, requirement structure, RAID review, status drafts and incident summaries; validate numbers/owners/dates and keep consequential decisions human-owned.
